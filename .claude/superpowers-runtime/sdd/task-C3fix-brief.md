## Deliverable 1 — disjoint finite coverage, writer → persistence → join → every ideate consumer (TDD)
1. `labelmaker.events.coverage`: add `finite_intervals(t_s, y=None, *, min_gap_s) -> tuple[tuple[float, float], ...]` — the maximal runs of finite samples (any-channel-finite for 2-D `y`, as `finite_span` does), merging runs separated by a gap SHORTER than `min_gap_s`; the corpus' trailing NaN sample (every fast group is 2^k+1 long, last sample NaN) and leading NaN are simply outside; all-NaN → `()` (the interval analogue of `UNKNOWN`). Add `intersect_intervals(*sets)` for multi-input heuristics (L→H = D-alpha ∧ n̄ₑ ∧ pinj at pipeline.py:1167–1169; the QH proxy's intersection of its required inputs from L-D2) and `union_intervals` where a step's coverage is "any channel". Keep `finite_span` for callers that need a display hull, but NO source row's coverage may be derived from it any more (grep every `finite_span`/`feature_spans`/`intersect` call in pipeline.py: ELM clock :1128, sawtooth :1147, L→H :1167–1169, the features/actuator rows :440, and the `ran[...] = span` assignments).
2. `min_gap_s` per source is a named constant beside the detector it guards, derived from that detector's own resolution and written down (e.g. the ELM clock's smoothing/min-distance in `transients.py`; the sawtooth inversion window; the actuator hysteresis minimum of 20 ms) — NOT tuned to any data. Persist it with the row (see 3) so a reader knows how small a gap could hide. Tests: a gap shorter than `min_gap_s` does not split; a longer one does; the trailing NaN never creates a spurious interval.
3. Persistence (`schema.SOURCE_COLUMNS`, `schema._source_row`, `coverage.source_records`/`_row`, `schema.write_sources/read_sources`): the disjoint intervals must be stored. Pick ONE design and justify it in the report: (a) one row per interval with an `interval` index column — then the key `(source, diag, channel, pass_name)` is no longer unique and EVERY reader that assumes uniqueness (both packages) must be found and changed; or (b) an `intervals` column (JSON list of `[t0, t1]` pairs, plus `min_gap_s`) beside `t_cov0_s/t_cov1_s`, which then remain the hull for DISPLAY only and are never used to decide coverage. Whichever you choose, the ideate join (`src/ideate/labels/join.py`, `event_sources` table) carries the intervals through unchanged.
4. Consumers decide from the INTERVAL SETS, not the hull: `ideate.labels.event_sources` (`CoverageSummary.from_rows`, `coverage_state`, `covers`, `coverage_span`, `observing_rows`, `shot_summary`), `retrieval/phenomena._coverage_for` (:807, and `evidence`, `locate`), `retrieval/describe`, `mcp/tools.get_events` (`_n_covering`, the `_NO_DETECTION_CAVEAT` at :614, the `uncovered` caveats). The hull must not re-enter through any fallback to event rows' `t_cov0_s/t_cov1_s` — find every such fallback on the ideate side and make it interval-aware or remove it (curated-table rows stay `UNKNOWN`/NaN as today — pipeline.py:1290, :1376).
